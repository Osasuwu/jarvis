- **S3** [HIGH] On protected repos, the HIGH/CRITICAL hold depends on two things the AC leaves open. The label has to go on at `gh pr create` time, and the AC doesn't say so. — `Osasuwu/jarvis/.github/workflows/waiting-human-review.yml` (`on.pull_request.types`); `docs/research/implement-skill-redesign-2026-09-30.md` finding 4

## Unmentioned alternatives
- **S4** Put the checkable rules in deterministic carriers instead of skill prose. Options: a PreToolUse deny on `gh pr merge --admin`; a `pr-body-check.yml` job that fails without a risk line and applies `waiting-human-review` on HIGH/CRITICAL; afk-label gating in the `agent-dispatch.yml` job `if` and prompt. This is likely missed because the redesign is framed as "rewrite SKILL.md". The user's own carrier order says rules that can be checked on the artifact or at the tool-call boundary "should never be prose" (`baseline-carriers.md` §Selection order). The trade-off is per-repo workflow edits in 3–4 repos versus prose that the audit shows decaying after compaction.
- **S5** Re-inject the skill after compaction, which is Claude Code's documented remedy: re-invoke the skill, or use a `SessionStart`/`compact` hook to re-inject the late-phase sibling file. This is likely missed because AC1 treats the 5,000-token per-skill cut as the only limit. The same docs paragraph adds a shared 25,000-token budget that is filled most-recent-first, so "older skills can be dropped entirely". `/grill` runs in the same session as `/implement` in 60 of 72 `grill_required` exits. The trade-off is that the hook is untested and does not run headless. Source: `02-external-evidence.md:145`, `03-citation-audit.md:191`.
- **S6** Measure the post-merge check against the baseline the audit already has: Risk Assessment presence and merge-rule compliance on runs with 4+ compactions, compared with the audit's 49/79 and 26/55. This is likely missed because "no comparative experiment" was taken to mean "no comparison". The before-numbers already exist at no cost (brief finding 1). The AC12 invariants instead cover AC1 (a static token count), the `decisions.md` line (already 56/59), and branches/re-read. None of these is the measured decay that motivated the redesign. The trade-off: 6 runs may hold few heavily compacted runs, so the check needs a compaction-count floor to mean anything.

## Challenged assumption
- **S7** The assumption that getting the body under 4,000 tokens, with must-not-skip rules first, is what brings back late-phase compliance (PR risk line, merge rule, terminal state). The documented mechanism doesn't guarantee re-attachment at any size: the shared 25,000-token budget is filled most-recent-first and can drop `/implement` entirely after `/grill` or other skills run in the same session. The external evidence the brief cites ("Lost in Compaction", `02-external-evidence.md:148`) concludes that keeping process constraints "requires architectural separation rather than better compactor prompts". AC1 also doesn't say where the PR template and merge text go once they're cut to fit: the body, which is re-attached, or sibling files read at the moment of use, which are not. So the main fix rests on an untested causal link: shorter file, therefore the late-phase rules survive.
